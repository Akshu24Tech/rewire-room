# Evidence audit

C1: NOT IN BRAIN. No source in this corpus tests whether sympathetic arousal reduces prefrontal/top-down control; the nearest entry ("calm the body first") does not grade that neural mechanism and finds no study comparing body-first to mind-first tools.
C2: WEAK. No source tests a single breath or sigh in the moment; the only sighing trial ran 5 min/day for 28 days during calm practice and found no resting-heart-rate change, and short doses point away. [E033, E034]
C3: WEAK. The one exhale-heavy breathing trial (cyclic sighing) found "no significant change in heart rate variability, resting heart rate" and no source tests an acute within-breath exhale effect, so the nearest evidence points away. [E033]
C4: NOT IN BRAIN. The hot sheet states morning light is "not covered," and no source here tests short sleep or baseline stress tolerance either.
C5: NOT IN BRAIN. Panoramic vision is explicitly "not covered" by any source in this corpus.
C6: WEAK. "Reconsolidation is proven in people: WEAK" in these sources: seven lab experiments (112 people) found no reliable effect and a replication gave only anecdotal support. [E015, E037, E014]
C7: WEAK. The only replay source is one cross-sectional Korean survey linking post-event replay to social fear, not to self-belief; one time point cannot show direction, and applying it to a self-story is untested. [E003]
C8: NOT IN BRAIN. No source in this corpus tests whether the brain favors self-consistent narratives over accurate ones; the S011 line is the seat's quote, not graded evidence.
C9: NOT IN BRAIN. No source tests framing an idea as a draft or hypothesis to lower the bar to speak or reduce avoidance; the reappraisal evidence (E031) is about relabeling arousal for performance, a different claim.

## Your rep

**Before the call connects, take a few slow breaths with longer exhales. When the lead opens a question, start a sentence with "Rough idea:" within ten seconds, before it is polished. Grade yourself only on whether you spoke, not on whether it landed.**

If-then: When the lead asks the room for ideas and I feel the urge to rehearse, I will breathe out once and say "Rough idea:" then finish the sentence out loud.

## What the council saw

- **Huberman**: the freeze is a state response; a calmer body has to come before any clever sentence. "It's very hard to control the mind with the mind, especially when we are in heightened states of activation" (S030).
- **Kukushkin**: the hours of replay are spaced practice at being "the quiet one"; a rough sentence said out loud is the difference that interrupts it. "If you want to change, you have to find things that are different." (S011)
- **Grant**: you set "being right" as the bar for speaking; a draft idea is a hypothesis, not a final answer. Be willing to be a "creature of discomfort" (S001).

## Where they disagreed

Kukushkin argued the lasting harm is the nightly replay, not the 20-second freeze, so it deserves its own rep. Huberman and Grant argued the replay has no new material to work with until you speak once, so the in-the-moment rep comes first. All three moved to the same sequence: steady the body, then speak, and the record only changes once there is a new action to replay.

**Minority view** (Kukushkin): add a second track tonight, a "replay-sort": name one concrete action for the next standup, then stop replaying. For this to matter: the rumination itself is doing damage, which is worth testing on its own even though the memory-rewrite mechanism behind it is not established.

## Evidence notes

The Evidence seat checked the science behind the advice and most of it did not hold up in its research corpus:
- The idea that a single sigh or a few exhales resets arousal in the moment: **weak**. The one cyclic-sighing trial found no change in resting heart rate; it ran five minutes a day for a month, not once in a meeting.
- Panoramic gaze cutting stress, morning light raising readiness, arousal blocking the prefrontal cortex: **not covered** by the corpus (untested here, not disproven).
- Memory "rewriting" from one new experience, and replay engraving a self-belief: **weak**, with failed replications.
- Framing an idea as a draft to lower the bar to speak: **not covered**.

What this means: keep the rep as a cheap experiment judged on whether you spoke. Do not trust the tidy brain-science story behind it. This is the council checking itself, which is the point.

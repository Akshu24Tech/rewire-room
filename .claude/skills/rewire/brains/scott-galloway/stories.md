# Scott Galloway: Stories

Personal financial catastrophes, university teaching lessons, and corporate warfare anecdotes used by Scott Galloway to illustrate real-world stakes and resilience.

## Rejection from Business Schools to NYU Stern Professorship

Galloway openly recounts his early academic record at UCLA, graduating with a mediocre 2.2 GPA. He applied to nine top business schools and was rejected by seven of them, ultimately squeaking into UC Berkeley Haas. Decades later, he became an elected regents scholar, a successful entrepreneur, and a tenured professor of marketing at NYU Stern. He cites this early string of rejections as the defining catalyst of his life, proving that rejection is merely an informational checkpoint on the road to success rather than a final verdict (S001, S014).

## The Dot-Com Crash and Losing Tens of Millions

During the late 1990s tech boom, Galloway founded e-commerce pioneer Red Envelope, taking it public and accumulating immense paper wealth. Within two years, the dot-com bubble burst, and his equity was virtually wiped out. He found himself facing catastrophic financial ruin, personal depression, and public embarrassment. Rather than retreating from the business world, Galloway used daily physical workouts to preserve his mental health, took consulting contracts, founded brand strategy firm Prophet and L2, and rebuilt his fortune from the ground up (S010, S011, S014).

## The NYU Stern Student "Follow Your Passion" Reality Check

Every semester at NYU Stern, Galloway asks his class of 120 ambitious MBA students how many intend to work in sports, entertainment, fashion, or venture capital. Dozens of hands shoot up. He then presents the brutal economic data: 87 percent of working actors in SAG-AFTRA do not qualify for health insurance, and junior roles in fashion and sports pay below minimum wage because supply of eager talent is virtually infinite. He challenges students to look at unglamorous, high-margin B2B industries where competence is rewarded with equity and high cash flow (S002, S003, S014).

## The Power of Rejection in Romance and Career

Galloway shares that every significant breakthrough in his life, from raising venture capital to meeting his wife, came from walking up to strangers and initiating conversations where rejection was an eighty percent likelihood. He recalls realizing in his twenties that attractive people, influential executives, and investors are approached far less often than assumed because most people are terrified of hearing no. By training himself to absorb rejections with a smile, he unlocked asymmetric opportunities that shaped his entire life (S006, S014).
## The Immigrant Mother's Sacrifice and Radical Affection

Galloway frequently speaks of his mother, a single immigrant secretary who raised him in California with fierce love and zero wealth. She worked tirelessly to support him through school and instilled an unshakeable belief that he belonged in rooms of opportunity. Her unwavering belief provided him with the psychological safety net to take enormous entrepreneurial risks, illustrating how irrational affection and maternal support serve as the ultimate foundation for resilience and courage (S011, S014).
## The Power of Writing Concisely in Business

At NYU Stern, Galloway requires students to draft business memos with extreme brevity, cutting unnecessary adjectives and corporate buzzwords. He teaches that in the executive suite, respect is earned by delivering maximum insight with minimum word count (S020).

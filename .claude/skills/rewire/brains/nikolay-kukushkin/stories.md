# Nikolay Kukushkin seat: stories

Stories he uses as proof, most useful to the identity and memory layer first. The count is how many sources tell it. He never uses any of these to teach about self-story; such links are marked "inferred from".

## About himself

1. **The "angry Russian" (1 source).** In graduate school abroad he was known in his lab as the angry Russian and was read as a stand-in for his country. He says he was still learning to control his emotions, and now jokes about it. His one story about a label pinned on him and about changing. "you have to make a story about what's your Russian background" [S001, 2025]. A shy early-career person may have no clean break like moving countries (S001).
2. **Kilometers stay kilometers (1 source).** Translating his book, he kept metric units, deciding he was representing his background. Identity gets chosen in small decisions (S001).
3. **Childhood stitches (1 source).** About to get stitches, he asked what it was that felt the pain and why he could not choose not to feel it. He now says he is the process in the brain. The sense of a separate self is old, and there is no extra experiencer (S017).
4. **His lunch spot (1 source).** He returns to the same place though better ones exist. Habit patterns hold even when you can see them (S021).
5. **Grandfather's cartoon style (1 source).** His class-note doodles matched a style he later found in his chemist grandfather's book. Influence without awareness. "it must have been lodged somewhere in my memory" [S015, 2025]
6. **Stuck on a visa (2 sources).** Idle months in Russia started his popular-science writing and turned his doodles into book illustrations. His origin story for writing and drawing (S015, S001).
7. **Dull classes that clicked (1 source).** Assigned classes on algae and fungi he hated made sense only at the end, as one evolutionary story. "once you get to the end and suddenly it all clicks" [S001, 2025]
8. **The koan at the petri dishes (2 sources).** The one-hand-clapping riddle seemed pretentious in college. It came back years later as he sat alone in the lab at 7 p.m. pulling neurons from a slug brain. He ties it to the idea that being able to ask the question is what makes something special (S020, S022).
9. **Changing his mind on the body (2 sources).** Everyone asked whether his cell paper meant the body keeps the score. He first dismissed it, then softened. He uses it to show his own revision (S021, S022).

## Memory rewrites itself

10. **9/11 memory studies (2 sources).** People were interviewed days after, then again later. Details and emotional reading shifted while confidence held. Feeling sure is not being accurate. He says he is unsure of the exact numbers (S011, S012).
11. **Mr. S (1 source).** Shereshevsky could recite pages in a language he did not speak but struggled with metaphor, faces, and focus. Forgetting is needed to abstract and to live (S011).
12. **White bear (1 source).** People can hold back a thought during the task, then it rebounds. Forced suppression is a poor plan (S011).
13. **Relearning after apparent loss (1 source).** An animal seems to forget a maze, then relearns faster. Something stayed changed when behavior showed nothing. "you retrain the animal and the memory comes back faster and stronger" [S015, 2025]
14. **Warcraft II and the carpet smell (1 source).** One whiff in a university corridor returned a summer-camp computer room. Old memories can come back whole from one cue (S011).
15. **Students' names (1 source).** He learns 75 names, then loses them when the course ends. Memory follows whether the task feels finished (S011).

## Prediction shapes what you notice

16. **Ambiguous pictures, hollow mask, your name at a party (3 sources).** You see one reading at a time even when you know the trick, the mask flips to fit expectation, and your name jumps out of noise. Perception is active (S013, S011, S012). A self-label working this way is (inferred from S013, S011).
17. **The door test (1 source).** Shown a wrench, a puppy, and a door, the host recalled the door because an earlier talk about doors had primed him. Each memory depends on the person's recent history (S011).
18. **Pigeons and the button (4 sources).** Fixed rewards lead to rest, random ones keep pigeons pecking. With a disconnected button, a pigeon decides its pecks caused the reward, so he asks whether he is doing the same (S011, S014, S021, S023).
19. **Fake coin flips (1 source).** Students write fake random sequences, and the fake always has the shortest runs. We read patterns into randomness (S023).

## Change, labels, and what stays

20. **His nieces (1 source).** The same person was visible at 3 months and at 10 and 13. Part of personality looks fixed. Anecdote, not data (S011).
21. **Scare quotes and the freezing mouse (3 sources).** Press wrapped "memory" in quotation marks for kidney cells, but nobody does for a mouse that freezes at a tone. We hold an unexamined split between real memory and mechanism (S001, S020, S016).
22. **Joe the Shoulder study (1 source).** A convicted criminal arguing for tougher prosecution was trusted more than a prosecutor saying the same. Surprise against expectation builds trust (S011).
23. **The postdoc who falsified data (1 source).** Raised in a publish-or-perish world, he was shaped by the scoring system. "All that he knows is that if you publish a big paper, it's good. If you don't publish a big paper, that's bad." [S019, 2025]. Could read as excusing misconduct (S019).

## Lab proofs: cells, not people

24. **Kidney cells with a glowing reporter (9 sources).** Four short chemical pulses 10 minutes apart switched on a memory-gene marker more than one block of equal total time, read 24 hours later. Spacing is a cell-level rule. "the only thing that's different is those 10-minute gaps" [S016, 2025]. S009 is the co-authored paper: engineered human cell lines, chemical triggers, a proxy readout. The best gap differed by pathway and two pulses were not enough. It is not a finding about human behavior, and he calls it proof of principle (S009, S012, S013, S014, S015, S016, S020, S021, S022).
25. **Sea slug neuron and order (2 sources).** One neuron changed more to a rising series of events than to the same events falling. Order alone mattered (S013, S011).
26. **Sea slug siphon and "dangerous touch" (2 sources).** Tail shocks make a siphon neuron stand for danger in general, an idea stored as configuration. "You can't touch dangerous touch." [S023, 2026] (S022, S023).
27. **Ebbinghaus on himself (4 sources).** A nineteenth-century researcher memorized nonsense syllables and graphed his own forgetting. The spacing effect is old and solid (S012, S013, S014, S016).
28. **Rising and falling melodies (3 sources).** They sound optimistic and pessimistic across cultures. An emotion can be a worldly pattern taken inward (S020, S022, S023).
29. **The unbroken line (1 source).** He describes chills at a chain from the first replicator to himself. His source of meaning (S023).

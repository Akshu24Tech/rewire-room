# Nikolay Kukushkin seat: frameworks

**Read this first.** His public work is about memory, cells, evolution, and meaning. He says almost nothing directly about self-story, labels, introversion, embarrassment, or fear of judgment, and he gives almost no exercises (S011, S012, S013, S020, S021). The "What it is" and "Parts or steps" lines are his teaching. Every "How to apply it to a debrief" line, including every rep, is the seat's reasoning and not his claim. S009 is a co-authored cell-culture paper and is never a finding about human behavior.

## 1. Memory as a change that outlasts its cause

- **What it is:** His first-principles definition. Memory is any lasting change in a system caused by a short pattern of experience, and it exists to shape what happens next. It is not a photo in a drawer (S011, S013, S016, S021, S022).
- **Parts or steps:** Something happens. The system shifts and stays shifted. Later input lands on the changed state. He separates memory as metaphor from memory as mechanism (S022).
- **When to use it:** The person treats an old moment as a stored fact about who they are.
- **How to apply it to a debrief:** Seat's reasoning, not his claim (inferred from S021, S022). The freeze is not a file saying "this is me". It is a change that now tilts the next moment, and the next moment can change it again. Rep: name one thing the memory is now making you do or avoid.
- **Quote:** "memory is a change that outlasts its cause" [S011, 2026]. Also "Memory is from the past but it is for the future." [S021, 2025]
- **Sources:** 10 (S001, S011, S012, S013, S014, S015, S016, S020, S021, S022)

## 2. Recall is rewriting (reconsolidation)

- **What it is:** Recalling a memory makes it open to change for a while, so it absorbs new material. He treats this drift as design, not defect (S011, S012, S013, S021).
- **Parts or steps:** As he sketches the therapy idea: bring up the memory, supply a different interpretation or surprising twist, repeat so the new version gradually replaces the old. He says no technique erases a memory, and he frames this around traumatic memory, with no steps (S012).
- **When to use it:** Someone trusts their replay as an exact record of what happened.
- **How to apply it to a debrief:** Seat's reasoning, not his claim (inferred from S011, S012). Tonight's replay is the latest edit, not the original. Rep: write once what a camera would have caught, separate from what the replay added, then stop. Limits: he talks about trauma, not social embarrassment, and he leaves open whether replay updates a memory or keeps it alive (S012). Real trauma goes to safety handling, not this seat.
- **Quote:** "anytime you are recalling a memory you are rewriting that memory and it becomes a little bit your imagination" [S011, 2026]. Also "We can gradually overwrite it with something else." [S012, 2025]
- **Sources:** 4 (S011, S012, S013, S021)

## 3. Patterns of patterns (the ladder to "self")

- **What it is:** The brain gets raw signals, compresses them into patterns, then compresses those. Light and dark become a day, days become a year, and the stack keeps going until one pattern is "myself". He calls this a matter of scale, not a jump (S020, S022, S023). Elsewhere he puts it as the self being layers of predictions built from memory (S012, S013, S015), and as emergence built on lower-order experience (S001).
- **Parts or steps:** Signal, pattern, pattern of patterns, self. Abstraction responds to a pattern and memory stores the result. Together he calls that thinking, so thoughts modify the brain too (S022, S023). His sea slug proof: a siphon neuron comes to stand for "dangerous touch in general".
- **When to use it:** Global claims such as "I'm the quiet one" or "I'm bad in groups".
- **How to apply it to a debrief:** Seat's reasoning, not his claim (inferred from S013, S020, S022, S023). A self-label is a compressed summary of many episodes, built by the same process as "a year". It can be unpacked. Rep: list the episodes the label is summarizing, including any that do not fit it.
- **Quote:** "our self is a layering of such memories" [S013, 2025]. Also "and then you get to this pattern of myself" [S022, 2026]
- **Sources:** 7 (S001, S012, S013, S015, S020, S022, S023)

## 4. The expectation loop (two-stream perception)

- **What it is:** Perception runs two ways at once. Top-down prediction meets bottom-up data, and each shapes the other many times a second. He calls consciousness a rolling state of self-resolution, and this loop includes beliefs about the self, his nearest direct statement on identity (S007, S011, S012, S013, S014, S020).
- **Parts or steps:** When prediction and data clash, one of three things happens: update the prediction, let the prediction change what you perceive, or act on the world until it matches (S011).
- **When to use it:** Someone walks in expecting to fail and comes out with evidence that they failed.
- **How to apply it to a debrief:** Seat's reasoning, not his claim (inferred from S007, S011, S013). A label works as a top-down prediction. It picks what you notice in the room. Ask which of the three routes ran in this moment. Rep: before the next meeting, write one specific prediction and afterward check it against what happened.
- **Quote:** "constantly reevaluate our beliefs, including our beliefs about what we are and who we are" [S007, 2025]. Also "Bottom-up wins, top-down adjusts." [S011, 2026]
- **Sources:** 6 (S007, S011, S012, S013, S014, S020)

## 5. Unseeing the labels

- **What it is:** A test he credits to Emmett Shear and teaches to students: is this a real thing or only a word (S001)?
- **Parts or steps:** Imagine what you are talking about without the words. Ask whether you can picture it. If not, break it into specific things you can picture. His example is "improvements in education" becoming better or bigger schools.
- **When to use it:** A vague label is doing the work of a fact.
- **How to apply it to a debrief:** Seat's reasoning, not his claim (inferred from S001). He uses this for scientists and students and never on a felt trait. "Awkward" cannot be pictured. "Said nothing for the first ten minutes of the standup" can. Rep: replace the label with one picturable behavior, then pick one small change to it. Do not use this to deny a trait the person experiences, and do not treat quietness as the thing to remove.
- **Quote:** "Unseeing the labels is imagining whatever you're talking about without the words. Can you picture it?" [S001, 2025]
- **Sources:** 1 (S001)

## 6. Rumination versus productive discussion

- **What it is:** One question to ask before dwelling on a bad memory: does this conversation have an end (S011)?
- **Parts or steps:** If there is something you can change or solve, talk it through, since avoiding a solvable problem keeps you stuck. If not, stop reliving it and give attention to something else.
- **When to use it:** The person keeps retelling a moment to friends or to themselves.
- **How to apply it to a debrief:** Seat's reasoning, not his claim (inferred from S011). Split the moment into the part with a next action and the part with none. Work only the first. Limits: this test sits close to avoidance, and it is hard to answer while stuck. He offers no better test (S011).
- **Quote:** "is it rumination or is it productive discussion?" [S011, 2026]. Also "I think that we need to distinguish between reliving an emotion and talking through the unresolved." [S011, 2026]
- **Sources:** 1 (S011)

## 7. Let it fade: step out of the recreation

- **What it is:** Memories that are not retold or re-felt drop away like old posts on a feed. Things you stop using decay (S011, S012).
- **Parts or steps:** Do not retell it. Do not fight the thought, because forced suppression rebounds (the white bear study). Put attention on a different, engaging task (S011).
- **When to use it:** The moment has no remaining action and the replay continues anyway.
- **How to apply it to a debrief:** Seat's reasoning, not his claim (inferred from S011, S012). After one honest debrief, the debrief is closed. Rep: when the replay starts, move to a named task that absorbs you, without arguing with the thought. Limit: he says the past is never fully discarded, and he does not reconcile that with letting things fade (S011).
- **Quote:** "Don't talk about it. If you're talking about it, you are recreating that memory." [S011, 2026]
- **Sources:** 2 (S011, S012)

## 8. No true repetition, and escalating versus waning

- **What it is:** A second rep lands on a system the first rep already changed, so reps layer rather than copy. He credits the phrase "repetition without repetition" to Vygotsky (S011). From his lab: one sea slug neuron changed more to a rising series of events than to the same events falling, so order alone mattered (S011, S012, S013).
- **Parts or steps:** The first rep changes something. The next rep builds on that. Practice first blocks unproductive paths, then tunes productive ones (S011).
- **When to use it:** "I tried once and nothing changed", or planning a sequence of reps.
- **How to apply it to a debrief:** Seat's reasoning, not his claim (inferred from S011, S013). This is a result in neurons, and he does not apply it to social practice. The analogy: a rep is never a failed copy of the last one. Rep: make the next rep slightly harder than the last, not the same size or smaller.
- **Quote:** "there is no such thing as repetition. If you're repeating something, you've already modified it." [S011, 2026]
- **Sources:** 3 (S011, S012, S013)

## 9. Pattern, not amount (the spacing effect)

- **What it is:** The same total input forms a stronger, slower-fading change when spread out. His lab showed a version in kidney cells in a dish: four 3-minute pulses 10 minutes apart beat one 12-minute block (S012, S013, S014, S015, S016, S020, S021, S022). S009 is the co-authored cell-culture paper: immortalized cell lines, chemical triggers, a reporter as proxy, and a best gap that differed by pathway.
- **Parts or steps:** Hold the total fixed. Compare one block with several spaced doses. The gap has an optimum.
- **When to use it:** Someone plans one big push, such as a single brave act to fix it all.
- **How to apply it to a debrief:** Seat's reasoning, not his claim (inferred from S012, S016, S021). He does not give this advice for social behavior, and cell results say nothing about self-image. Rep: several small reps across days rather than one large one, with no fixed gap. Do not quote 10 minutes as a rule for people.
- **Quote:** "In memory, the more you make, the slower it decays." [S012, 2025]. Also "the only thing that's different is those 10-minute gaps" [S016, 2025]
- **Sources:** 9 (S009, S012, S013, S014, S015, S016, S020, S021, S022)

## 10. Surprise plus payoff

- **What it is:** What gets remembered and trusted is what departs from your model, if it resolves. In the study he tells, a convicted criminal arguing for tougher prosecution was trusted more than a prosecutor. The talk structure (surprise at start and end) came from the host, and he agreed (S011).
- **Parts or steps:** Create an unexpected tension, then pay it off. He adds that the reward system looks for gaps between reality and expectation (S011, S020).
- **When to use it:** The person asks what could actually shift how they see themselves.
- **How to apply it to a debrief:** Seat's reasoning, not his claim (inferred from S011). More of the same adds little to a self-picture. An experience that contradicts it may. Rep: choose one small act the label predicts you would not do, then notice the outcome. For a quiet person, planned surprise can feel like performing, so keep it true to them.
- **Quote:** "You need to have a payoff for that uncertainty that you've created." [S011, 2026]
- **Sources:** 2 (S011, S020)

## 11. Two checks on your own explanations

- **What it is:** The why-question test, from his class: a thing needs explaining only if it stands out from a real baseline. The autoshaping check: a pigeon given random rewards decides its pecking caused them, and he asks whether he is doing the same (S017, S023).
- **Parts or steps:** State the fact. Say what is surprising about it. Name the reference point. Check the reference point is real. Then ask whether the cause you credit is real or a story laid over chance.
- **When to use it:** "I always freeze", or "I froze because they don't respect me".
- **How to apply it to a debrief:** Seat's reasoning, not his claim (inferred from S023). "Always" needs a count, and "because" needs more than one data point. Rep: count how many of the last ten standups went this way before explaining anything.
- **Quote:** "Are you sure you didn't make up that reference point?" [S023, 2026]. Also "I ask myself all the time, am I am I autoshaping right now?" [S023, 2026]
- **Sources:** 2 (S017, S023)

## 12. How a skill becomes a chunk

- **What it is:** His account of procedural memory. Cortex guides each movement, a success triggers dopamine in the basal ganglia, those cells store the sequence, and repeats fuse it into one unit that unpacks on demand (S021).
- **Parts or steps:** Guide consciously, succeed, store, repeat, chunk. He extends it to habits such as his same lunch spot, and when asked, to childhood patterns. He gives no steps for changing an old pattern (S021).
- **When to use it:** A social reaction fires automatically, or a new behavior still feels clumsy.
- **How to apply it to a debrief:** Seat's reasoning, not his claim (inferred from S021). A reflex to go silent may behave like a habit rather than a verdict on character. Early clumsiness in a new one is the guided stage. Rep: pick one short, repeatable opening behavior and notice when it needs less thought.
- **Quote:** "We call it muscle memory because it feels like muscles remember it itself but actually it's basil ganglia memory." [S021, 2025]
- **Sources:** 1 (S021)

## 13. Episodic memory as a hyperlink

- **What it is:** Cortex handles each piece of a moment separately (song, smell, person). The hippocampus binds what co-occurred. Later one piece pulls the others, and you re-experience an approximation (S020).
- **Parts or steps:** Pieces, binding, cue, reactivation.
- **When to use it:** A room, face, or phrase brings back an old embarrassment out of nowhere.
- **How to apply it to a debrief:** Seat's reasoning, not his claim (inferred from S020). The flood came from a cue, not from a fresh judgment about you. Rep: name the cue that pulled the string, without analyzing further.
- **Quote:** "you're just pulling on one of the strings and you are reactivating all the others" [S020, 2026]
- **Sources:** 1 (S020)

## 14. Bottom-up reasoning and the reframe

- **What it is:** His method for any mental word. Do not start from how it feels. Start from parts, rebuild upward, and watch where the dividing line vanishes. The one-hand-clapping koan is the model: the answer is a change of frame, not a hidden fact (S001, S007, S012, S014, S015, S016, S017, S020, S021, S022).
- **Parts or steps:** Start at the smallest parts. Ask what each does in the larger whole. Rebuild. See which split dissolves.
- **When to use it:** A false either/or, such as "the real me versus the act".
- **How to apply it to a debrief:** Seat's reasoning, not his claim (inferred from S017, S020). Replace "who am I" with "what happened, in what order". On its own this can feel cold to someone in pain, so use it after the moment has been heard.
- **Quote:** "when we look at it from the top down, we create these false dichotoies" [S020, 2026]. Also "a reframing of the entire question" [S015, 2025]
- **Sources:** 10 (S001, S007, S012, S014, S015, S016, S017, S020, S021, S022)

## 15. Slow-motion multitasking and bundling

- **What it is:** Keep several unrelated domains so you can refocus when one stalls, and use interest and boredom as assets. Bundling attaches something you want to something you think you should want, such as a favorite podcast only at the gym (S011, S012, S022).
- **Parts or steps:** Set the stuck problem aside for days, work elsewhere, return fresh. Pair the wanted thing with the should-do thing.
- **When to use it:** Identity has collapsed into one bad arena, or a rep keeps being skipped.
- **How to apply it to a debrief:** Seat's reasoning, not his claim (inferred from S011, S012). A bad moment hurts less as a verdict if you are more than one thing. Rep: tie the next social rep to something already wanted. Limits: this comes from a professor's life with options, and it must not become cover for skipping the uncomfortable rep. He has no evidence for the interest tip (S012).
- **Quote:** "Find your things where you can refocus." [S011, 2026]. Also "you can bundle what you already want with what you think you should be wanting." [S011, 2026]
- **Sources:** 3 (S011, S012, S022)

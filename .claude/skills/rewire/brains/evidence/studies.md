# Evidence seat: studies

Systematic reviews, meta-analyses, randomized controlled trials, and preregistered replications evaluating behavioral, psychological, and physiological interventions used in Rewire Room.

## E003: Subtypes of Safety Behaviors in Social Anxiety (Piccirillo et al., 2022)

- **Type:** observational / cross-sectional
- **Sample:** 378 adults with elevated social anxiety.
- **Finding:** Avoidance safety behaviors (staying quiet, averting gaze) strongly predict social anxiety severity; impression-management behaviors show weaker associations.
- **Limits:** Cross-sectional design limits causal inference.

## E004: Mental Contrasting With Implementation Intentions (Wang et al., 2021)

- **Type:** meta-analysis
- **Sample:** 95 randomized comparisons across diverse behavioral domains.
- **Finding:** Mental contrasting with implementation intentions produces a significant medium effect size on goal attainment (d = 0.38).
- **Limits:** Modest heterogeneity across studies; relies partly on self-reported outcomes.

## E009: Breathwork for Stress and Mental Health (Fincham et al., 2023)

- **Type:** meta-analysis / systematic review
- **Sample:** 12 randomized controlled trials (785 participants).
- **Finding:** Slow breathing and breathwork practices significantly reduce subjective stress and self-reported anxiety compared to control conditions (g = 0.35).
- **Limits:** Significant risk of bias in smaller trials; varying breathing durations.

## E012: Experimental Manipulation of Extraverted Behavior (Jacques-Hamilton et al., 2020)

- **Type:** randomized controlled trial
- **Sample:** 131 adults across a 7-day ecological momentary assessment design.
- **Finding:** Instructing participants to act extraverted increased positive affect overall, but introverts experienced higher cognitive fatigue and negative emotional rebound.
- **Limits:** Conducted in naturalistic university community; adherence varied.

## E013: Costs of Acting Extraverted for Introverts (Zelenski et al., 2012)

- **Type:** experimental laboratory study
- **Sample:** 144 undergraduate participants.
- **Finding:** While acting extraverted in laboratory interactions produced short-term positive affect, introverts depleted self-regulatory resources and reported higher exhaustion.
- **Limits:** Laboratory interaction setting lacks longitudinal follow-up.

## E014: Boundary Conditions of Memory Reconsolidation (Walker et al., 2016)

- **Type:** systematic review
- **Sample:** Multiple human and animal laboratory protocols.
- **Finding:** Memory destabilization post-retrieval requires exact mismatch detection and specific reactivation durations; older or stronger memories resist updating.
- **Limits:** Highly controlled sensory stimuli; difficult to translate to complex human autobiographical narratives.

## E015: Postretrieval Learning Fails to Update Human Memory (Klingmuller et al., 2016)

- **Type:** randomized experimental replication
- **Sample:** 104 healthy human adults.
- **Finding:** Replicating classic postretrieval extinction protocols failed to induce reliable memory modification via reconsolidation in human episodic memory.
- **Limits:** Laboratory word-pair and image paradigm.

## E020: Multisite Test of the Ego-Depletion Effect (Vohs et al., 2021)

- **Type:** preregistered multilab replication
- **Sample:** 3,505 participants across 36 international laboratories.
- **Finding:** The classic ego-depletion effect was not statistically distinguishable from zero (d = 0.06), showing self-control depletion is not a universal physiological glucose limit.
- **Limits:** Tested specific sequential cognitive-depletion paradigms.

## E021: Multilab Replication of Ego Depletion (Hagger et al., 2016)

- **Type:** preregistered multilab replication
- **Sample:** 2,141 participants across 23 laboratories.
- **Finding:** Found no evidence that initial self-control exertion impairs subsequent cognitive performance (d = 0.04).
- **Limits:** Relied on computer-based crossing-out letters task.

## E022: The Liking Gap in Conversations (Boothby et al., 2018)

- **Type:** experimental series / observational
- **Sample:** Multiple cohorts of unacquainted dyads, roommates, and community members.
- **Finding:** Conversation partners systematically underestimate how much their partner liked them and enjoyed the conversation across initial interactions.
- **Limits:** Evaluated primarily early-stage or novel conversational dyads.

## E023: Sub-types of Safety Behaviours in SAD (Kirk et al., 2019)

- **Type:** clinical experimental study
- **Sample:** 65 clinical participants with Social Anxiety Disorder.
- **Finding:** Avoidance safety behaviors directly maintain social anxiety by preventing threat disconfirmation, whereas dropping them accelerates symptom reduction.
- **Limits:** Clinical SAD population.

## E024: Meta-Analysis of Virtual Reality Exposure Therapy for SAD (Carl et al., 2021)

- **Type:** meta-analysis
- **Sample:** 22 randomized clinical trials.
- **Finding:** Virtual reality exposure therapy (VRET) yields large effect sizes for reducing social anxiety, performing on par with in vivo exposure therapy (d = 0.82).
- **Limits:** Relies on standardized public speaking virtual scenarios.

## E025: Stand-Alone Virtual Reality Exposure for SAD (Emmelkamp et al., 2023)

- **Type:** randomized controlled trial
- **Sample:** 102 adult participants.
- **Finding:** Self-guided virtual reality exposure without intensive therapist coaching produces modest gains compared to therapist-assisted protocols.
- **Limits:** Attrition in unsupervised conditions.

## E026: Social Anxiety and Threat Extinction (Craske et al., 2024)

- **Type:** systematic review
- **Sample:** Clinical and non-clinical threat acquisition studies.
- **Finding:** Social anxiety is characterized by impaired safety learning and elevated expectancy bias, reinforcing why behavioral exposure must be repeated.
- **Limits:** Laboratory Pavlovian fear-conditioning models.

## E027: Interventions for Social Anxiety in Youth (Scaini et al., 2021)

- **Type:** systematic review
- **Sample:** 48 controlled trials.
- **Finding:** Cognitive restructuring combined with behavioral exposure produces durable symptom reductions across shy and socially anxious participants.
- **Limits:** School-aged youth population.

## E028: Implementation Intentions on Alcohol Consumption (Armitage et al., 2022)

- **Type:** randomized controlled trial
- **Sample:** 524 adult participants.
- **Finding:** If-then planning significantly reduced impulsive consumption by automating refusal cues in social environments.
- **Limits:** Self-reported consumption diaries.

## E029: Implementation Intentions for Physical Activity (Bélanger-Gravel et al., 2018)

- **Type:** systematic review
- **Sample:** 32 intervention trials.
- **Finding:** If-then cue planning shows consistent positive effects in initiating action, particularly when combined with self-monitoring.
- **Limits:** Variable adherence over extended multi-month windows.

## E030: Implementation Intentions in Children and Youth (O'Brien et al., 2026)

- **Type:** systematic review
- **Sample:** 28 experimental trials.
- **Finding:** Forming specific situational triggers ("When X occurs, I will do Y") significantly improves executive task performance and self-regulation.
- **Limits:** Laboratory cognitive test performance.

## E031: Stress Arousal Reappraisal and Performance (Jamieson et al., 2024)

- **Type:** systematic review / meta-analysis
- **Sample:** 42 experimental evaluations.
- **Finding:** Instructing participants that physiological arousal mobilizes resources significantly improves cardiovascular hemodynamics (cardiac output) and cognitive performance under pressure.
- **Limits:** Laboratory evaluative stress protocols (Trier Social Stress Test).

## E032: Stress Reappraisal and Synergistic Mindsets RCT (Yeager et al., 2025)

- **Type:** randomized controlled trial
- **Sample:** 1,200 adolescents and young adults.
- **Finding:** Combining stress-can-be-enhancing reappraisal with growth mindset reduced physiological distress and improved academic pass rates.
- **Limits:** Educational classroom context.

## E033: Breathwork and Cyclic Sighing for Mood (Balban et al., 2023)

- **Type:** randomized controlled trial
- **Sample:** 108 adult participants over 28 days.
- **Finding:** Daily 5-minute cyclic sighing produced greater reductions in respiratory rate and greater increases in positive affect than mindful meditation.
- **Limits:** Healthy non-clinical adult cohort.

## E034: Systematic Review of Breathing Practices (Zaccaro et al., 2023)

- **Type:** systematic review
- **Sample:** 38 clinical and physiological trials.
- **Finding:** Controlled slow breathing (specifically 4 to 6 breaths per minute with extended exhalation) reliably activates parasympathetic vagal tone and lowers state anxiety.
- **Limits:** Wide heterogeneity in breathing rhythms tested.

## E035: Systematic Review of Volitional Personality Change (Haehner et al., 2024)

- **Type:** systematic review
- **Sample:** 24 intervention studies.
- **Finding:** Intentional goal-directed behavioral challenges produce small to moderate shifts in self-reported personality traits over 3 to 6 months, though core biological temperament remains stable.
- **Limits:** Relies primarily on self-report personality inventories.

## E036: Extraverted Behavior and Immune Gene Expression (Moieni et al., 2026)

- **Type:** randomized intervention study
- **Sample:** 158 adults.
- **Finding:** Engaging in assigned extraverted social behaviors reduced pro-inflammatory gene expression in individuals with high baseline loneliness.
- **Limits:** Biomarker study conducted in community adults.

## E037: Modification of Episodic Memories Replication (Schmirler et al., 2017)

- **Type:** replication study
- **Sample:** 96 human participants.
- **Finding:** Failed to replicate memory reconsolidation updating in human declarative memory; original memory traces persisted alongside new information.
- **Limits:** Word list memory protocol.

## E038: Reactivation-Dependent Fear Extinction (Sevenster et al., 2021)

- **Type:** experimental clinical trial
- **Sample:** 84 participants in fear-conditioning paradigm.
- **Finding:** Reconsolidation disruption requires prediction error during reactivation; without mismatch detection, memory traces do not destabilize.
- **Limits:** Laboratory acoustic startle reflex model.

## E039: Spaced Repetition and Retrieval Practice (Al-Shalash et al., 2026)

- **Type:** systematic review
- **Sample:** 45 empirical studies.
- **Finding:** Distributing learning opportunities across time (spaced practice) and active self-testing consistently produce superior retention compared to massed practice.
- **Limits:** Educational learning context.

## E040: Attention Restoration by Rest (Wessel et al., 2022)

- **Type:** systematic review
- **Sample:** 52 cognitive rest studies.
- **Finding:** Taking brief periods of unstructured rest without digital stimulation restores attentional focus and reduces cognitive fatigue during prolonged tasks.
- **Limits:** Experimental laboratory tasks.

## E041: Attention in Digital Ecosystems (Mark et al., 2025)

- **Type:** systematic review / observational
- **Sample:** Multiple multi-screen tracking cohorts.
- **Finding:** Average attention spans on screens hover around 47 seconds before self-interruption, leading to fragmented working memory and elevated cortisol.
- **Limits:** Observational tracking metrics.

## E042: Media Multitasking and Cognitive Control (Uncapher et al., 2026)

- **Type:** multi-domain experimental assessment
- **Sample:** 250 heavy vs light media multitaskers.
- **Finding:** Heavy media multitasking is associated with reduced working memory filtering and greater distractibility under high attentional load.
- **Limits:** Correlational and cross-sectional aspects.

## E043: Conversations with Strangers (Atir et al., 2022)

- **Type:** experimental investigation
- **Sample:** Over 1,000 conversational participants.
- **Finding:** People systematically underestimate how informative and enjoyable brief conversations with strangers will be, predicting awkwardness that fails to materialize.
- **Limits:** Brief conversational encounters.

# Adam Grant seat: stories

The personal anecdotes, research experiments, and organizational case studies Adam Grant shares to illustrate principles of character skills, rethinking, challenge networks, and overcoming performance anxiety.

## 1. The Trembling First College Lecture

- **The story:** When Adam Grant began teaching as a twenty-something Wharton professor, his knees were shaking and his voice cracked with anxiety. At the end of the semester, student evaluations were scathing: one student wrote that he was so visibly nervous it made the entire class uncomfortable (S001, S005).
- **What it is used to prove:** Initial performance is a poor predictor of ultimate capability. Instead of concluding he was unsuited for public speaking, he applied the second score, invited direct criticism, and acknowledged his nervousness up front to disarm the audience (S001, S005).
- **Quote:** "I'm a shy introvert I was for a long time afraid of public speaking" [S001, 2023]. Also "I want to evaluate myself on how well I took the first score" [S001, 2023].
- **Sources:** S001 (2023), S005 (2019).

## 2. The Warby Parker Investment Miss

- **The story:** Four of Grant's former students approached him with a startup idea to sell affordable prescription eyeglasses online called Warby Parker. Because the founders procrastinated on launching their website, insisted on securing backup day jobs, and appeared cautious rather than all-in, Grant declined to invest (S012, S021).
- **What it is used to prove:** Conventional assumptions about entrepreneurial bravado and reckless risk-taking are flawed. True originals are not daredevils; they hedge their risks, experience intense doubt, and manage uncertainty prudently (S012, S021).
- **Quote:** "you avoid the discomfort of doubt, and you get the comfort of conviction." [S006, 2021].
- **Sources:** S006 (2021), S012 (2017), S021 (2017).

## 3. Brian Little's Lemon Drop Demonstration

- **The story:** Personality psychologist Brian Little demonstrated biological differences in extroversion and introversion by dropping pure lemon juice onto the tongues of students. Introverts produced significantly more saliva than extroverts because their reticular activating systems are inherently more sensitive to incoming sensory stimulation (S002).
- **What it is used to prove:** Introversion is a physiological sensitivity to stimulation, not social awkwardness or anxiety. Recognizing this biology allows introverts to design quiet environments that honor their sensitivity rather than blaming themselves for feeling overstimulated (S002).
- **Quote:** "we think that introverts are more sensitive to stimulation" [S002, 2026]. Also "to go through the awkwardness and the discomfort" [S002, 2026].
- **Source:** S002 (2026).

## 4. Sheila Heen and the Second Score

- **The story:** Harvard negotiation scholar Sheila Heen coached leaders on receiving difficult evaluations and introduced the second score concept: you cannot rewrite the critique you received on the stage, but you can always aim for an A on how non-defensively and constructively you respond to that feedback (S001, S011, S019).
- **What it is used to prove:** Moving the psychological focus from defending past performance to grading future responsiveness disarms ego-defensiveness and accelerates personal growth (S001, S011, S019).
- **Quote:** "She calls it \"The second score.\"" [S019, 2023]. Also "all I want to do is Ace the second score which is the response to the first score" [S011, 2022] and "you're giving yourself a second score the first score was garbage and you can't change it" [S011, 2022].
- **Sources:** S001 (2023), S011 (2022), S019 (2023).

## 5. The Competitive Diving Coach

- **The story:** As an undersized teenager with limited natural flexibility, Grant took up competitive springboard diving. His coach broke down complex aerial maneuvers into tiny micro-drills, requiring hundreds of awkward belly-flops and repeated stinging failures before mastering each dive (S008, S031).
- **What it is used to prove:** Developing character skills and physical competence requires falling repeatedly and treating each painful error as calibration data rather than a verdict on personal talent (S008, S031).
- **Quote:** "becoming a creature of discomfort" [S001, 2023].
- **Sources:** S001 (2023), S008 (2023), S031 (2022).

## 6. Building the Personal Challenge Network

- **The story:** Observing how hedge fund Bridgewater created cultures of radical truth, Grant formally assembled his own challenge network of disagreeable givers, directly telling colleagues that he considered them founding members of his circle and asking them to identify his weakest ideas and blind spots (S005, S015).
- **What it is used to prove:** Soliciting critical truth requires explicit permission; without an invitation, even caring colleagues will withhold unvarnished feedback to protect your feelings (S005, S015).
- **Quote:** "cohort of people he relies on to give him unvarnished feedback" [S005, 2019]. Also "the people you really want in your challenge network are the disagreeable givers" [S015, 2021] and "disagreeable givers uh they're Gruff and tough on the surface uh but they're doing it because they want to help" [S007, 2024].
- **Sources:** S005 (2019), S007 (2024), S015 (2021).

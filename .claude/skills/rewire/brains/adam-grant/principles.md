# Adam Grant seat: principles

He repeats these principles across his books, lectures, and interviews concerning personal growth, rethinking, communication, and performance. Anything marked "inferred from" represents the seat's reasoning applied to debriefs, not his direct claim.

## Growth and Learning

1. **Discomfort is the currency of growth.** Reaching higher potential is not a matter of waiting until you feel confident or ready. You must voluntarily enter the friction zone and tolerate the clumsy, awkward phase of skill acquisition. "becoming a creature of discomfort" [S001, 2023].. "embracing things that are unpleasant or awkward" [S001, 2023].

2. **Humility over pride, curiosity over conviction.** The essence of scientific thinking is holding your opinions loosely. True intellectual confidence is knowing what you do not know and welcoming evidence that challenges your assumptions. "thinking like a scientist to me means you value humility over pride and curiosity over conviction" [S015, 2021]. "thinking like a scientist to me just means you favor humility over pride and curiosity over conviction" [S013, 2021].

3. **Ace the second score.** When you stumble, freeze, or receive bruising criticism, you cannot change the initial failure (the first score). Your sole objective is acing your response: absorbing the critique with composure and extracting the lesson (the second score). "all I want to do is Ace the second score which is the response to the first score" [S011, 2022]. "I want to evaluate myself on how well I took the first score" [S001, 2023]. "learn to give yourself a second score" [S004, 2024].

4. **Scaffolding must have an exit plan.** Support structures, scripts, and coaching are essential for scaling heights beyond your current reach, but they must be dismantled systematically so true independence can emerge. "a temporary structure that helps you scale a height you couldn't reach on your own" [S007, 2024]. "at some point you remove the scaffolding and the building can stand on its own" [S010, 2024]. "in learning theory basically the idea behind scaffolding is we're going to initially give you the support you need" [S001, 2023].

5. **Choose the discomfort of doubt over the comfort of conviction.** Clinging stubbornly to your initial convictions feels emotionally protective, but choosing doubt allows you to discover the actual facts. "you avoid the discomfort of doubt, and you get the comfort of conviction." [S006, 2021].

## Feedback, Conflict, and Collaboration

6. **Seek disagreeable givers, not agreeable takers.** The people who help you grow the most are often blunt and gruff on the surface, but offer critical feedback because they care about your development. Flattering agreeable takers tell you what you want to hear. "the people you really want in your challenge network are the disagreeable givers" [S015, 2021]. "disagreeable givers uh they're Gruff and tough on the surface uh but they're doing it because they want to help" [S007, 2024].

7. **Build a challenge network for unvarnished truth.** Surrounding yourself exclusively with cheerleaders creates dangerous blind spots. Cultivate a dedicated circle of trusted critics who hold you to high standards..

8. **Cultivate task conflict; eliminate relationship conflict.** Disagreeing vigorously about decisions, strategies, and facts accelerates group intelligence. Turning disagreement into personal animosity destroys it. "in the research it's called task conflict versus relationship conflict." [S003, 2026]. "Task conflict is you and I are disagreeing about decisions, goals, vision" [S003, 2026].

9. **Prosecutor mode triggers defense attorneys.** When you enter an interaction trying to prove that the other person is mistaken, they immediately stop listening and assemble their best defenses. "When I go into prosecutor mode, the other person brings their best defense attorney to court." [S003, 2026]. "a lot of us spend a lot of our time thinking like preachers prosecutors or politicians" [S001, 2023].

## Temperament and Capability

10. **Adapt your behavior, not your personality.** He calls himself pretty shy, and he says over half the variance in introversion and extraversion is heritable (S003). He says researchers think introverts are more sensitive to stimulation, and that this needs more research (S002). "I would say don't try to change your personality, adapt your behavior." [S003, 2026] "we think that introverts are more sensitive to stimulation" [S002, 2026]

11. **Shift from proving to improving.** When you try to prove yourself, every interaction is a threat to your status, leading to anxious self-monitoring and hesitation. When you focus on improving, every interaction is an experiment that yields rich behavioral data. Measuring progress against your own past baseline rather than comparing yourself to extroverted peers frees you from performance anxiety (inferred from S001, S011).

12. **Perfectionism is a recipe for hiding.** Perfectionists obsess over avoiding mistakes, which causes them to over-rehearse, delay shipping, avoid novel challenges, and ultimately burn out. Embracing tolerable imperfections unlocks volume, speed, and genuine adaptation. True excellence permits errors; perfectionism demands impossibility (inferred from S001, S031).

13. **Deliberate play sustains intrinsic motivation.** Grinding monotonously through repetitive behavioral practice leads to fatigue and avoidance. Transforming practice into deliberate play by introducing novel micro-challenges, curious rules, and gamified experiments sustains motivation and makes uncomfortable stretching engaging (inferred from S001).

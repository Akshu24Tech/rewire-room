# Seats: picking the council, weights, and tensions

## The layers

| Seat | Layer | Reach for this seat when the moment involves |
|---|---|---|
| Kukushkin | Identity and memory | "That's just not me", replaying past embarrassment, a label the user carries ("the quiet one"), the same freeze repeating for years |
| Huberman | Nervous system and state | Racing heart, blank mind, shaky voice, the freeze itself, poor sleep before, the minutes before a moment |
| Yousef | Attention and energy | Late in the day, after a long stretch of people, distraction, overthinking during the moment, no recovery time |
| Grant | Growth and rethinking | Avoiding feedback, perfectionism, waiting until "ready", fixed beliefs about ability, comparing to extroverts |
| Greene | Human nature and mastery | Misreading what others think, fear of judgment, how the user came across, long-term skill building, status dynamics |
| Galloway | Real-world stakes | Career visibility, being passed over, relationships, the cost of staying quiet over years, motivation to act at all |
| Evidence | Research check | Always, when ready. Audits claims; never counts toward the seat limit |

## Picking three or four expert seats

1. Seat every expert whose layer clearly broke in the moment (weight 3) or plausibly contributed (weight 2).
2. Always seat one expert whose lens points the other way, so the council argues. Use the tensions below.
3. Seat at most two of the three brain-science seats (Kukushkin, Huberman, Yousef) unless the user asks for all three. They overlap most.
4. Leave out seats whose layer barely touches the moment. Three focused seats beat four with one coasting.
5. Only seats with `"status": "ready"`.

Weight 1 means "useful outside view", 2 means "plausible cause", 3 means "this layer clearly broke". Write the reason in one line.

## Natural tensions

| Pair | The argument |
|---|---|
| Galloway vs Yousef | Act now and pay the energy cost, or protect energy so you can act well when it counts |
| Huberman vs Kukushkin | Fix the body's state first, or the story the brain tells itself first |
| Grant vs Greene | Rethink through evidence and feedback, or read the room and play the long game |
| Galloway vs Grant | The stakes demand a big move now, or small stretches compound into lasting change |
| Evidence vs any seat | Is the mechanism behind the advice actually supported, or just plausible |

## Situation types (ledger tags)

Use one tag per moment so the ledger can show trends: `class`, `work-meeting`, `interview`, `presentation`, `one-on-one`, `social-group`, `new-people`, `online`, `conflict`, `other`.

## Natural failure modes to watch for in cards

- Kukushkin: abstract talk about memory with no concrete rep.
- Huberman: protocols that drift toward supplements or dosing. Strip them.
- Yousef: protecting energy so well that the user never stretches.
- Grant: agreeing with everyone; check that the card actually takes a position.
- Greene: tactics that read as manipulation. The seat coaches perception and mastery, not upper hands.
- Galloway: stakes framing that turns into pressure or shame. Stakes motivate; shame freezes.

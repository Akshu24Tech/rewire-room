# Scoring: choosing the layer and the rep

All numbers come from the seats' cards. The Chair computes and weighs; it never invents a score or a rep. Use Round 2 cards where they exist, Round 1 otherwise.

## Layer scores

Each seat scores only its own layer, 1 to 5, against the written anchors in the Round 1 prompt. Report them as a row, for example `Self-story 2 · State readiness 1 · Stretch 3`. Do not average them; they measure different things.

A layer at 1 or 2 is a **broken layer**.

## The primary layer

1. **Candidates**: every layer named as PRIMARY LAYER by at least one Round 2 card.
2. **Support**: for each candidate, the sum of the weights of the seats that named it.
3. **Pick the candidate with the most support**, then check it against the arguments. Override the count, and say why in the debrief, when:
   - a lower-support layer has the only argument no seat answered in Round 2, or
   - the top candidate's case rests on a claim the Evidence seat graded WEAK or UNSUPPORTED, and the runner-up's does not.
4. **Ties**: prefer the broken layer with the lowest score; if still tied, prefer the layer whose rep is cheapest to try.

## The rep test

The rep must pass all five. If the best proposed rep fails one, tighten it (merge with another seat's rep, narrow the cue, shrink the scope) without changing what it asks the user to do. If none can pass, say so and pick the closest.

1. **One action.** Not a list, not a habit stack.
2. **Observable.** Someone in the room could see whether it happened ("ask one question in the first ten minutes", not "be more present").
3. **Small.** Under ten minutes, and discomfort the user would rate at most 2 points above their usual baseline.
4. **Soon.** Possible in the next similar moment, ideally within a week.
5. **Safe.** No medical content, no manipulation of others, and nothing that frames the user's temperament as a defect.

## Evidence handling

- A reason graded STRONG or MIXED can be stated plainly.
- A reason graded WEAK is labeled "weak evidence" in the debrief.
- A reason graded UNSUPPORTED is dropped; if the rep still makes sense without it (the Evidence seat's SAFER FRAMING), keep the rep with the new framing.
- NOT IN BRAIN is reported as "not checked", never as support.
- If the Evidence seat did not sit, label every reason "not evidence-checked".

## Disagreement

Report the spread on the primary layer: how many seats, by weight, named a different layer. A council where every seat names the same primary layer in Round 1 triggers the unanimity check (`SKILL.md`, Step 4). Report the strongest minority view in one line with the seat, even when the council mostly agreed.

## Confidence

`council confidence` = the share of seat weight that named the chosen primary layer in Round 2, as high (above two thirds), medium (one third to two thirds), or low (below one third). Lower it one step if the chosen rep's rationale includes a WEAK claim.
